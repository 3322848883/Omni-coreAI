# F2: Forward trading vs backtest — walk-forward, paper-trading limits, overfitting, multi-strategy significance, live-selection failure modes

## Findings

### [1] Walk-forward (WFA) performance is highly sensitive to the chosen train/test window lengths, so window size must itself be optimized and reported, not fixed arbitrarily.
- quote: "the performance of a trading strategy using the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) evaluated within a walk-forward procedure based on the Robust Sharpe Ratio is highly dependent on the chosen window size. We investigated the strategy on intraday Bitcoin data at six frequencies (1 minute to 60 minutes) using 81 combinations of walk-forward window lengths (1 day to 28 days) over a 19-month training period."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10785
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-02-11
- confidence: high

### [2] Double out-of-sample design (global training period + a never-touched unseen period used exactly once) is required to stop "repeated optimizations on data designated as out-of-sample" from destroying OOS integrity.
- quote: "a common but often unreported issue involves repeated optimizations on data designated as out-of-sample, or the complete omission of an out-of-sample evaluation phase. Such practices foster unrealistic performance expectations, leading to substantial underperformance when strategies are implemented in live trading environments."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10785v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-02-11
- confidence: high

### [3] On Bitcoin intraday, a walk-forward EMA strategy that beat Buy-and-Hold in every training window only matched Buy-and-Hold out of sample; the gain that survived was lower drawdown and a 50% MDD cut when blended with Buy-and-Hold, not raw alpha.
- quote: "In the training period for Bitcoin, all combinations of walk-forward windows outperformed a Buy-and-Hold strategy. During the testing period, the strategy performed similarly to Buy-and-Hold but with lower drawdown and a higher Information Ratio. ... a portfolio combining Buy-and-Hold with our strategies outperformed all individual strategies ... achieving the highest overall performance and a 50 percent reduction in drawdown."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10785
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-02-11
- confidence: high

### [4] Transaction costs of 10 bps flip naive sign-based ML crypto strategies from profitable to losing on ~70k hourly BTC/USDT bars; a cost-aware execution filter (trade only when |forecast| exceeds a cost-proportional threshold) restores net profitability in selected configurations.
- quote: "All three models produce positive gross trading performance in selected configurations, but naive sign-based strategies fail once transaction costs of ten basis points are imposed. A cost-aware execution filter ... sharply reduces turnover and restores profitability in selected configurations."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00060
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-05-19
- confidence: high

### [5] Even under a 27-fold walk-forward protocol, architecture ranking (XGBoost vs LSTM vs iTransformer) is not statistically significant after bootstrap multiple-testing correction — descriptive leaders are not proven superiors.
- quote: "XGBoost is descriptively stronger than the neural alternatives, although bootstrap evidence does not support formal statistical dominance. Loss-function and model-selection effects are secondary and statistically fragile."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00060v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-05-19
- confidence: high

### [6] A deliberately leaky oracle with Sharpe ratio 35 survives Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Probability of Backtest Overfitting tests completely — statistical deflation does not catch look-ahead; structural leakage prevention and statistical correction are complementary, not substitutes.
- quote: "a deliberately leaky oracle posting a Sharpe ratio of 35 survives Deflated Sharpe and probability-of-backtest-overfitting testing completely. ... the two guardrails are complementary, not substitutes."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27734v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-27
- confidence: high

### [7] Autonomous multi-strategy search must deflate reported performance by a recorded trial ledger; best in-sample Sharpe climbs with each trial while the DSR deflation threshold (driven by N trials and Sharpe dispersion V) climbs faster, so unlogged search budgets are a bias amplifier.
- quote: "the system records every strategy evaluation its search performs and deflates all reported performance by that trial count, tracing how the best in-sample Sharpe ratio climbs with each trial while the deflation threshold, driven by the agent's own search, climbs faster."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-27
- confidence: high

### [8] Under honest evaluation (DSR + PBO + stationary-bootstrap CIs + paired vs B&H), passive benchmarks are certified while every LLM-discovered strategy is rejected across 2 frontier models, search budgets up to 100 candidates, and 5 repeated runs — three named failure modes: selection luck, predicted rank degradation, out-of-sample collapse.
- quote: "honest evaluation certifies passive benchmarks (out-of-sample confidence intervals excluding zero), rejects every LLM-discovered strategy (across two frontier models, search budgets up to one hundred candidates, and five repeated runs), catching selection luck, predicted rank degradation, and out-of-sample collapse through complementary instruments"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27734
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-27
- confidence: high

### [9] Pre-registered hypotheses earn a much lower multiple-testing bar than brute search; power arithmetic shows certifying moderate low-frequency edges requires decades of OOS data or substantial breadth — directly relevant to 15–20 parallel personas on short paper-trading windows.
- quote: "The framework formalizes why a pre-registered hypothesis faces almost no deflation while a broad search faces a high bar, and the statistical-power arithmetic quantifies why certifying moderate low-frequency edges requires decades of out-of-sample data or substantial breadth."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27734v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-27
- confidence: high

### [10] A deployment-grade protocol is IS → purged rolling WFA → OOS holdout with parameter lock after WFA, plateau (not single-optimum) parameter selection (Ω_stable = {θ | SR(θ) ≥ 0.9·SR_opt}), and pre-committed majority-pass + catastrophic-veto gates; OOS must not be opened if WFA fails.
- quote: "each fold i is deemed PASS only if the metric vector on W_i^test meets the minimum benchmark b; the entire WFA is PASS if the proportion of passing folds meets threshold q (majority-pass), and FAIL immediately upon triggering the catastrophic veto ... OOS is not opened upon WFA FAIL."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09219v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-03-10
- confidence: high

### [11] Selecting alphas by peak in-sample Sharpe is a live-capital failure mode: v1 had the highest IS Sharpe (2.97 fold1 / 3.18 WFA mean) but the weakest OOS; ranking also reverses when the objective switches from maximizing Sharpe to minimizing MaxDD (v3 Sharpe 2.61 vs v4 MaxDD 4.21%).
- quote: "Control observation: v1 has the highest IS Sharpe but the weakest OOS—illustrating the risk of selecting alphas by IS peak. ... The rank reversal when switching from maximizing Sharpe to minimizing MaxDD reflects the fundamental trade-off between performance and tail-risk control"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09219v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-03-10
- confidence: high

### [12] Post-selection robustness gates (Deflated Sharpe Ratio, Probability of Backtest Overfitting, Superior Predictive Ability, Minimum Track Record Length + regime-stability) separate lucky backtests from true edge in synthetic markets (AUROC 0.989) but showed NO significant forward relationship on unseen real-market data (Spearman ρs=0.013, one-sided permutation p=0.40) — paper/OOS grades are not proof of live profit.
- quote: "In a pre-registered test on unseen real-market data, the score showed no significant forward relationship in a population with limited surviving edge (Spearman rho_s = 0.013, one-sided permutation p = 0.40). We therefore present the MinervaScore as an auditable validation and reporting layer, rather than as evidence of demonstrated real-market predictability."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23808v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-24
- confidence: high

### [13] Canonical multi-strategy instruments and their failure modes: DSR (Bailey & López de Prado 2014, JPM 40(5):94–107) corrects selection luck; PBO via CSCV (Bailey et al., J. Computational Finance 20(4):39–69) measures the chance the in-sample winner underperforms its own median OOS; SPA (Hansen 2005) / White Reality Check (Econometrica 68(5):1097–1126) correct benchmark luck; MinTRL addresses insufficient track length.
- quote: "The DSR gate addresses selection luck ... The PBO gate addresses selection instability: it asks whether the in-sample winner tends to fall below the median of its own candidate cohort out of sample. The SPA gate addresses benchmark luck ... The MinTRL gate addresses evidence length"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23808v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-08-24
- confidence: high

### [14] Crypto DRL specifically: estimating probability of overfitting and rejecting overfitted agents before deployment made less-overfitted agents outperform more-overfitted ones, equal-weight, and S&P DBM Index on 10 cryptocurrencies during the 05/01/2022–06/27/2022 double-crash test.
- quote: "we train the DRL agents, estimate the probability of overfitting, and reject the overfitted agents, increasing the chance of good trading performance. Finally, on 10 cryptocurrencies over a testing period from 05/01/2022 to 06/27/2022 (during which the crypto market crashed two times), we show that the less overfitted deep reinforcement learning agents have a higher return than that of more overfitted agents"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05559
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-09-12 (v6 2023-01-31)
- confidence: high

### [15] Bitcoin-strategy evaluation standards proposed after surveying the peer-reviewed literature: walk-forward evaluation, multi-regime holdout windows, naive-baseline comparison, inclusion of zero in hyperparameter grids, and Diebold-Mariano significance testing — "the field's primary need is not more models but better evaluation."
- quote: "We identify open research directions and propose concrete methodological standards for future work -- walk-forward evaluation, multi-regime holdout windows, naive baseline comparison, inclusion of zero in hyperparameter grids, and Diebold-Mariano significance testing -- arguing that the field's primary need is not more models but better evaluation."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00071
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-05-20
- confidence: high

### [16] Walk-forward is not universally endorsed: López de Prado (2019, SSRN 3459866) favors Monte Carlo with synthetic data over WFA because WFA assumes a consistent data-generating process and ignores stochasticity; LeBaron & Weigend (1998, IEEE TNN 9(1):213–220) showed train/validation/test splits overestimate performance and that bootstrap variability comes from the split, not the parameters.
- quote: "López de Prado (2019) favored Monte Carlo with synthetic data over walk-forward, as the latter assumes consistent data processes and ignores stochasticity. LeBaron and Weigend (1998) critiqued train/validation/test splits for overestimating performance, using bootstrapping to show variability from splits, not parameters."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10785v1
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-02-11
- confidence: medium
