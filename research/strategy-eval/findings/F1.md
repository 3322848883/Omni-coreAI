# F1: Trading strategy performance metrics standards and pitfalls

## Findings

### [1] Sharpe (1994) states the t-statistic of mean differential return equals the historic Sharpe ratio times the square root of the number of return observations T, linking raw SR to sample-size significance.
- quote: "The historic Sharpe Ratio is closely related to the t-statistic for measuring the statistical significance of the mean differential return. The t-statistic will equal the Sharpe Ratio times the square root of T (the number of returns used for the calculation)."
- url: https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/sr/sr.htm
- source_type: primary
- published: 1994 (Fall, Journal of Portfolio Management)
- confidence: high

### [2] Sharpe (1994) requires the ratio to be computed on differential (or excess) returns versus a benchmark; ranking strategies by raw return/σ can invert the correct ranking versus excess-return Sharpe.
- quote: "it is essential that the Sharpe Ratio be computed using the mean and standard deviation of a differential return ... the Sharpe Ratio provides the correct answer (a strategy using Y is preferred to one using X), while the 'return information ratio' provides the wrong one."
- url: https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/sr/sr.htm
- source_type: primary
- published: 1994 (Fall, Journal of Portfolio Management)
- confidence: high

### [3] Sharpe (1994) warns Sharpe does not incorporate correlation with an investor's other holdings, so it is insufficient alone when comparing strategies that change portfolio correlation.
- quote: "it is essential to remember that the Sharpe Ratio does not take correlations into account. When a choice may affect important correlations with other assets in an investor's portfolio, such information should be used to supplement comparisons based on Sharpe Ratios."
- url: https://web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/sr/sr.htm
- source_type: primary
- published: 1994 (Fall, Journal of Portfolio Management)
- confidence: high

### [4] Bailey & López de Prado (2014, JPM 40(5):94-107) define the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) as a Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio whose rejection threshold is inflated for multiple testing, and require disclosure of the number of independent trials N, variance of trial SRs, sample length T, skew and kurtosis.
- quote: "the most important piece of information missing from virtually all backtests published in academic journals and investment offerings is the number of trials attempted. Without this information, it is impossible to assess the relevance of a backtest. Put bluntly, a backtest where the researcher has not controlled for the extent of the search involved in his or her finding is worthless, regardless of how excellent the reported performance might be."
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2014-07-31 (preprint; JPM 2014-09-30)
- confidence: high

### [5] Bailey et al. (AMS Notices, May 2014) prove that after only N=10 independent strategy configurations the expected maximum in-sample Sharpe is 1.57 even when every configuration has true OOS Sharpe of zero.
- quote: "if the researcher tries only N = 10 alternative configurations of an investment strategy, he or she is expected to find a strategy with a Sharpe ratio IS of 1.57, despite the fact that all strategies are expected to deliver a Sharpe ratio of zero OOS"
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-pseudo.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2014-04-01 (preprint; Notices of the AMS May 2014, 458-471)
- confidence: high

### [6] Bailey et al. derive Minimum Backtest Length (MinBTL) ≈ 2 ln(N) / E[max_N]^2 years: with only 5 years of data, no more than 45 independent model configurations should be tried before an IS Sharpe of 1 is almost guaranteed noise; with 2 years, only 7 configurations.
- quote: "if only 5 years of data are available, no more than 45 independent model configurations should be tried, or we are almost guaranteed to produce strategies with an annualized Sharpe ratio IS of 1, but an expected Sharpe ratio OOS of zero. ... After trying only 7 independent strategy configurations, the expected maximum SR IS is 1 for a 2-year long backtest"
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-pseudo.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2014-04-01 (preprint; Notices of the AMS May 2014)
- confidence: high

### [7] Bailey et al. show the holdout / k-fold method cannot prevent backtest overfitting because it treats selection as a single trial; reapplying holdout ~20 times at 95% confidence makes false positives expected.
- quote: "the holdout method can not prevent backtest overfitting: Holdout assesses the generality of a model as if a single trial had taken place, again ignoring the rise in false positives as more trials occur. If we apply the holdout method enough times (say 20 times for a 95% confidence level), false positives are no longer unlikely: They are expected."
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2014-07-31
- confidence: high

### [8] Bailey & López de Prado (2012, Journal of Risk 15) introduce Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR) and Minimum Track Record Length (MinTRL); a 2-year monthly hedge-fund record with annualized SR 1.59, skew −2.448, kurtosis 10.164 has PSR≈0.55 under non-Normality and cannot be accepted at 95% confidence.
- quote: "An annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.59 over the last two years seems high enough ... when we incorporate the skewness ... and kurtosis information ... this yields a reassuring PSR(0)=0.9781 ... However, when we incorporate the skewness (−2.448) and kurtosis (10.164) information, then PSR(0)=0.5502!"
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/sharpe-frontier.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2012 (Journal of Risk vol. 15; SSRN 2011/2012)
- confidence: high

### [9] MinTRL for observed annualized SR=2 versus true-SR threshold 1 at 95% is 2.73 years (daily IID Normal), 3.24 years (monthly IID Normal), and 4.99 years under HFR-like monthly skew −0.72 / excess kurtosis 5.78 — non-Normality alone adds ~54% more required history.
- quote: "a 2.73 years track record is required for an annualized Sharpe of 2 to be considered greater than 1 at a 95% confidence level. ... the requirement is 2.83 years ... increases to 3.24 years if instead we work with monthly IID Normal returns ... the track record should now be 4.99 years long. This is 54% longer than what we required with Normal monthly returns"
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/sharpe-frontier.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2012 (Journal of Risk vol. 15)
- confidence: high

### [10] Bailey & López de Prado (2012) report that on HFR monthly indices 2000-2011 (134 months), only 9 of 28 styles have PSR(0.5)>0.95; many advertised high Sharpe styles (e.g. Quant Direct an. SR 0.506, MinTRL(0.5)≈77,399 years) are not statistically distinguishable from skill-less at SR>0.5.
- quote: "only 9 style indices substantiate investment skill over an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.5 at a 95% confidence level: Distressed Securities, Equity Market Neutral, Event Driven, Fixed Asset-Backed, Macro, Market Defensive, Mortgage Arbitrage, Relative Value, Systematic Diversified"
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/sharpe-frontier.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2012 (Journal of Risk vol. 15)
- confidence: high

### [11] A 2026 SSRN preprint (Choufani) shows Bailey–López de Prado MinTRL assumes finite fourth moment; for return tail index α∈(2,4) the CLT foundation fails and Gaussian MinTRL is inapplicable — median US equity α̂≈2.69 implies MinTRL ≈ 1,100 years for SR=0.5 at 95%, so standard 3–5 year due-diligence tracks have effectively zero statistical power under fat tails.
- quote: "At α̂ = 3.0, MinTRL ≈ 124 years—beyond any individual manager's career. At α̂ = 2.69 (the empirical median), MinTRL ≈ 1,100 years. ... standard due-diligence track-record requirements of three to five years have effectively zero statistical power for instruments in the empirically prevalent fat-tail regime"
- url: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2139/ssrn.7024958
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-07-29 (SSRN preprint)
- confidence: medium

### [12] Bailey, López de Prado & del Pozo (2012/2013) show selecting strategies solely on past Sharpe is suboptimal once correlation to the approved set is considered; a Sharpe-ratio indifference curve governs approval, and firms can even improve portfolio SR by hiring managers with negative expected performance if correlation effects dominate.
- quote: "selecting strategies (or portfolio managers) solely based on past Sharpe ratio will lead to suboptimal outcomes, particularly when we ignore the impact that these decisions will have on the average correlation of the portfolio. Our strategy approval theorem proves that, under certain circumstances, it is entirely possible for firms to improve their overall Sharpe ratio by hiring portfolio managers with negative expected performance."
- url: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2139/ssrn.2003638
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2012-02-15 (SSRN; Algorithmic Finance 2(2013) 99-109)
- confidence: medium

### [13] Sortino & Price (1994) is the canonical peer-reviewed source for downside-risk performance measurement (Journal of Investing 3(3):59-64, DOI 10.3905/joi.3.3.59); Sortino's later work (1991 JPM "Downside risk"; 1999 "Dutch Triangle") frames upside potential vs downside risk as the alternative to mean-variance SR.
- quote: "Performance Measurement in a Downside Risk Framework" (Sortino, Frank A.; Price, Lee N.) — Journal of Investing, vol. 3, issue 3, pages 59-64, published 1994-08-31
- url: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3905/joi.3.3.59
- source_type: secondary
- published: 1994-08-31
- confidence: medium

### [14] DSR's expected-maximum-SR inflation from multiple testing is analytic: E[max{SR̂}] ≈ μ + σ·((1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γΦ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e))) with Euler–Mascheroni γ≈0.5772; in a worked example an annualized SR̂=2.5 over T=1250 daily obs after N=100 trials yields DSR≈0.90 (not significant at 95%), while N=46 would pass at DSR≈0.9505.
- quote: "The analyst responds that N=100, Var[SR̂]=0.3, T=1250, γ̂=−0.72 and κ̂=5.78. Shortly after, the investor declines the analyst's proposal. ... there is only a 90% chance that the true SR associated with this strategy is greater than zero. Should the strategist have made his discovery after running only N=46 independent trials, the investor may have allocated some funds, as DSR would have been 0.9505"
- url: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf
- source_type: primary
- published: 2014-07-31
- confidence: high
