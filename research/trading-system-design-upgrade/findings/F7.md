# F7: Design and evaluation of multi-agent debate / consensus mechanisms for LLM decision making

Scope note: primary-source literature (arXiv) on debate protocols, voting/arbitration, and debate pathologies. All quotes below were read from pages actually fetched (arXiv abstract pages, arXiv full-text HTML, and the arXiv API metadata endpoint).

## Findings

### [1] The canonical "multiagent debate" protocol is N identical model instances that propose answers, then read each other's reasoning over multiple simultaneous rounds and revise toward a common answer, with no fine-tuning required.
- quote: "multiple language model instances propose and debate their individual responses and reasoning processes over multiple rounds to arrive at a common final answer"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-23 (Du, Li, Torralba, Tenenbaum, Mordatch)
- confidence: high

### [2] In a head-to-head benchmark across 7 datasets, multi-agent debate (MAD) did NOT reliably beat non-debate baselines: on MedQA, non-debate Medprompt scored 0.65 vs Society-of-Minds 0.64, Self-Consistency 0.60 and single-agent 0.60, while Multi-Persona scored 0.58; MAD protocols were also the most hyperparameter-sensitive.
- quote: "multi-agent debating systems, in their current form, do not reliably outperform other proposed prompting strategies, such as self-consistency and ensembling using multiple reasoning paths"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-11-29 (v1); v3 2024-07-18 (Smit, Duckworth, Grinsztajn, Barrett, Pretorius — InstaDeep)
- confidence: high
- note: Table 2 numbers (MedQA / PubMedQA / MMLU / CosmosQA / CIAR / GPQA / Chess): Medprompt 0.65/0.77/0.74/0.48/0.54/0.27/0.32; Society of Mind 0.64/0.74/0.73/0.44/0.56/0.27/0.26; Ensemble Refinement 0.64/0.74/0.76/0.45/0.48/0.32/0.32; ChatEval 0.60/0.75/0.71/0.45/0.48/0.26/0.32; Self-Consistency 0.60/0.74/0.78/0.46/0.56/0.24/0.27; Single Agent 0.60/0.75/0.76/0.45/0.50/0.33/0.27; Multi-Persona 0.58/0.70/0.72/0.46/0.52/0.29/0.33.

### [3] Debate quality is dominated by one tunable hyperparameter — the prompted "agreement intensity" ("you should agree with the other agents X% of the time") — which gave ≈15% accuracy gain for Multi-Persona and ≈5% for Society-of-Minds on USMLE, and the optimal direction is dataset-dependent (counter-intuitive CIAR tasks prefer disagreement).
- quote: "modulating the agreement intensity in this way provides a substantial (≈15%) improvement in performance for Multi-Persona, and (≈5%) for SoM on the USMLE dataset"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17371v3
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-07-18 (v3)
- confidence: high

### [4] The sequential-vs-simultaneous axis is an explicit design choice: ChatEval defines (a) one-on-one sequential turn-taking where each agent sees all prior agents' answers, (b) simultaneous-talk where agents generate responses asynchronously within a round specifically "to nullify the effects of agent order", and (c) simultaneous-talk-with-summarizer, which overwrites shared history with a per-round summary.
- quote: "simultaneous-talk, where agents asynchronously generate responses in each round to nullify the effects of agent order"
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17371v3
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-07-18 (Smit et al. §2 summarising Chan et al., ChatEval; Table 1 marks ChatEval as having Flexible Round Numbers, Sequential Interactions, Multi API Calls and Asymmetric Agents)
- confidence: high
- note: Smit et al.'s own reading of the mechanism: sequential turn-taking introduces order/position effects, which simultaneous-round generation is designed to cancel. In their benchmark ChatEval reached 0.60 on MedQA, i.e. no better than a single agent.

### [5] The cheap non-debate baseline to beat is self-consistency: sample many reasoning paths from one prompt and take the majority answer, worth +17.9% GSM8K, +11.0% SVAMP, +12.2% AQuA, +6.4% StrategyQA, +3.9% ARC-challenge over greedy chain-of-thought.
- quote: "self-consistency boosts the performance of chain-of-thought prompting with a striking margin on a range of popular arithmetic and commonsense reasoning benchmarks, including GSM8K (+17.9%), SVAMP (+11.0%), AQuA (+12.2%), StrategyQA (+6.4%) and ARC-challenge (+3.9%)"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-03-21 (v1), ICLR 2023 (Wang, Wei, Schuurmans, Le, Chi, Narang, Chowdhery, Zhou)
- confidence: high

### [6] Debate's clearest measured win is in the asymmetric "weak judge arbitrates strong experts" setting, not symmetric peer debate: a non-expert judge selecting between two debating experts reached 76% (model judge) and 88% (human judge) accuracy versus naive baselines of 48% and 60%, and optimising debaters for persuasiveness further improved judge accuracy.
- quote: "debate consistently helps both non-expert models and humans answer questions, achieving 76% and 88% accuracy respectively (naive baselines obtain 48% and 60%)"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-09 (v1), v4 2024-07-25 (Khan, Hughes, Valentine, Ruis, Sachan, Radhakrishnan, Grefenstette, Bowman, Rocktäschel, Perez)
- confidence: high

### [7] Debate can be actively harmful: accuracy can decline across rounds even when stronger models outnumber weaker ones, because models shift from correct to incorrect answers in response to peer reasoning and prefer agreement over challenging flawed reasoning; the authors attribute this to sycophancy and social conformity.
- quote: "debate can lead to a decrease in accuracy over time - even in settings where stronger (i.e., more capable) models outnumber their weaker counterparts"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05396
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-05 (Wynn, Satija, Hadfield; ICML MAS Workshop 2025)
- confidence: high
- note: second verbatim from same abstract: "models frequently shift from correct to incorrect answers in response to peer reasoning, favoring agreement over challenging flawed reasoning".

### [8] Inter-agent sycophancy is a first-class failure mode of debate systems: it collapses disagreement before the correct answer is reached, can leave the debate worse than a single-agent baseline, and has distinguishable debater-driven and judge-driven forms, in both decentralized and centralized (judge-arbitrated) frameworks.
- quote: "sycophancy is a core failure mode that amplifies disagreement collapse before reaching a correct conclusion in multi-agent debates, yields lower accuracy than single-agent baselines, and arises from distinct debater-driven and judge-driven failure modes"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23055
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-27 (Yao, Shang, Du, He, Lian, Zhang, Su, Swamy, Qi)
- confidence: high

### [9] Long debates fail by "problem drift" rather than by running out of rounds: drift affects 76–89% of generative tasks but only 7–21% of high-complexity tasks; human expert analysis of 170 drifting debates attributed it to lack of progress (35%), low-quality feedback (26%) and lack of clarity (25%), and the authors' DRIFTPolicy mitigation recovered only 31% of cases.
- quote: "generative tasks drift often due to the subjectivity of the answer space (76-89%), compared to high-complexity tasks (7-21%)"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19559
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-02-26 (v1); accepted EACL 2026 (Becker, Kaesberg, Stephan, Wahle, Ruas, Gipp)
- confidence: high

### [10] The two scaling knobs are not interchangeable: in legal-reasoning debate, increasing the agent population reduced inconsistency and improved accuracy (up to +8% over strong single-agent baselines), while extending the number of rounds induced a "detrimental over-deliberation drift" in which agents reinforce each other's mistakes.
- quote: "increasing the agent population reduces inconsistency and improves accuracy, whereas extending discussion rounds induces a detrimental over-deliberation drift where agents reinforce each other's mistakes"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09099
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-07-10 (Nguyen et al.; Outstanding paper, AI4Law Workshop @ ICML 2026)
- confidence: medium
- note: This is the most directly actionable result for a small deployment: buy accuracy with more (diverse) voters, not with more rounds.

### [11] Consensus-seeking debate has three named defects — conformity-driven error propagation (initially-correct agents get dragged to wrong answers), majority-vote randomness/unfairness in the final round, and multi-round token overhead — and a single-round, whole-trajectory-scoring, anti-conformity design (Free-MAD) reportedly beats them on 8 benchmarks while costing less.
- quote: "due to the inherent conformity of LLMs, agents that initially produce correct responses may be influenced by incorrect ones during the debate process, causing error propagation"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11035
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-14 (Cui, Fu, Zhang, Wang, Zuo)
- confidence: medium
- note: same abstract: "majority voting introduces randomness and unfairness in the decision-making phase, and can degrade the reasoning performance"; Free-MAD "requires only a single-round debate".

### [12] Stated consensus overstates real agreement: agents that publicly yield to a unanimous majority still internally represent their original premise in their residual stream (J-lens hit@100 0.85/0.22/0.24 for three open-weight models), and simply hiding an agent's own earlier answer changed conformity from 8% to 89% of questions in Qwen3.5-4B.
- quote: "Stated consensus in multi-agent debate can thus overstate agreement."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02702
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-10-02 (Ni & Zou; pre-registered)
- confidence: medium
- note: Implication for logging/audit: the final transcript is a weak record of what each agent actually computed; and whether an agent can see its own prior answer is a massive conformity lever.

### [13] Running debate on every query is the wrong default: selective triggering (iMAD, a classifier over self-critique hesitation cues) cut token usage by up to 92% while improving final accuracy by up to 13.5%, because unconditional debate "may even degrade accuracy by overturning correct single-agent answers".
- quote: "triggering MAD for every query is inefficient, as it incurs substantial computational (token) cost and may even degrade accuracy by overturning correct single-agent answers"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11306
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-11-14 (Fan, Yoon, Ji; AAAI 2026 Oral)
- confidence: high

### [14] Heterogeneity matters more than model strength or round count in the one clean positive result: after 4 rounds of debate, a diverse set of medium-capacity models (Gemini-Pro, Mixtral 8x7B, PaLM 2-M) scored 91% on GSM8K and beat GPT-4, whereas 3 instances of Gemini-Pro reached only 82%; the diverse set also set a then-SOTA 94% on ASDiv.
- quote: "after 4 rounds of debate, a diverse set of medium-capacity models (Gemini-Pro, Mixtral 7BX8, and PaLM 2-M) outperforms GPT-4 on the GSM-8K benchmark, scoring 91% accuracy. By comparison, when 3 instances of Gemini-Pro are used, performance only reaches 82%"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12853
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-10-10 (Hegazy; JRAR Vol. 5 Issue 3)
- confidence: medium
- note: The same paper claims "multi-agent debate helps at any model scale" and that the benefit is largest for diverse trained models.

### [15] Single-shot LLM judging is too noisy to arbitrate a debate: repeated identical evaluations flipped pairwise preferences 13.6% of the time on average (28% of questions above a 20% flip rate, worst case 56%), cross-judge agreement was only 76% (κ=0.51), and ~11 repeated trials were needed for majority vote to recover the 50-trial reference verdict at 95% probability (15 for high-variance questions).
- quote: "pairwise preferences flip on average 13.6% of the time, with 28% of questions exceeding a 20% flip rate and one question reaching 56%"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13685
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-04-23 (Yagubyan)
- confidence: medium
- note: Authors' recommendation: multi-trial aggregation, position randomisation, explicit uncertainty reporting as standard practice. Same page reports a first-position bias (72% A-majority, p=0.024) for GPT-4o-mini.

### [16] Even frontier judges are not self-consistent: on 650 questions, Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5 failed to maintain consistent pairwise preferences in nearly a quarter of difficult cases, and a panel-of-judges plus deep reasoning measurably improved judging consistency.
- quote: "even the top-performing models, Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5, fail to maintain consistent preferences in nearly a quarter of difficult cases"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16041
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-12-17 (Feng, Wang, Cheng, Wan, Chen et al.)
- confidence: medium

### [17] Persona design is a direct sycophancy control knob: across 13 open-weight models (0.6B–20B), 9 showed statistically significant positive correlation between a persona's agreeableness and its sycophancy rate, with Pearson r up to 0.87 and Cohen's d up to 2.33.
- quote: "9 of 13 models exhibit statistically significant positive correlations between persona agreeableness and sycophancy rates, with Pearson correlations reaching $r = 0.87$ and effect sizes as large as Cohen's $d = 2.33$"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10733
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-04-12 (Shah, Mishra, Silpasuwanchai; ACL 2026 Main)
- confidence: medium
- note: Directly relevant to a "multi-persona debate group": a warm/agreeable persona is measurably more likely to fold under pressure than a blunt/critical one.

### [18] Aggregation of independent judgements is a competitive substitute for debate: learned aggregators (logistic regression matching an MLP) beat all individual models and classical aggregation on 254 binary prediction-market questions, with a pure model-disagreement signal recovered as the lowest-complexity useful formula; separately, a 3-model "medoid" crowd chosen by behavioural clustering beat majority voting over all 25 models while cutting model calls by 88%.
- quote: "Learned aggregators -- a multilayer perceptron and a logistic regression -- outperformed all individual models and classical methods"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18269
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-05-26 (Douven); companion result arXiv:2608.24001 (Chetlapalli et al., 2026-08-25)
- confidence: medium
- note: Also from the same cluster of work: a 12-LLM ensemble was statistically indistinguishable from a crowd of 925 human forecasters on 31 binary questions (arXiv:2402.19379, Schoenegger, Tuminauskaite, Park, Tetlock, 2024-02-29).

## Dead ends
- `www.bing.com/search?q=...` returned localized Chinese dictionary/etymology results (baidu baike, Cambridge Dictionary) for English technical queries — unusable for this angle.
- `duckduckgo.com/html/?q=...` returned a transport error on every attempt.
- `mojeek.com/search?q=...` returned HTTP 403.
- The arXiv API works well but unquoted `all:` queries are noisy: `all:"wisdom of the crowd"` pulled in causal-discovery, emotion-recognition and reward-modelling papers with no debate relevance.
- No usable full-text access found for the ChatEval paper itself (cited here only as summarised inside Smit et al.'s fetched full text).
- I did not find a paper that reports an explicit "optimal round count" as a tuned scalar with a recommended default; the closest evidence is that rounds are the wrong knob (findings [10], [11], [13]).

## Suggested follow-ups
1. Does the debate/aggregation literature transfer when the decision is a continuous action (position size, leverage, entry timing) rather than a discrete multiple-choice answer — is verbal debate or weighted numeric aggregation better for that output type?
2. What is the measured effect of enforced disagreement quotas and explicit devil's-advocate roles on correct→incorrect flips in a sequential pipeline (offline benchmarks show the mechanism, but not the tuned operating point)?
3. For a per-bar decision cadence with a small account, is the cost-optimal configuration "self-consistency + selective debate trigger + multi-trial judge" rather than fixed rounds — and what is the marginal accuracy per dollar of each component?
