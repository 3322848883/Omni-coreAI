# F6: Engineering best practices for risk control and position sizing in automated crypto trading systems (per-trade risk budgeting, volatility targeting, drawdown limits and kill switches, daily loss limits, leverage/margin caps)

## Findings

### [1] Mature open-source bots implement risk limits as declarative "protection" objects that suspend trading per-pair or globally for a fixed duration, and the same protection can be instantiated several times at different thresholds to build short-term and long-term layers.
- quote: "Protections will protect your strategy from unexpected events and market conditions by temporarily stop trading for either one pair, or for all pairs." ... "Each Protection can be configured multiple times with different parameters, to allow different levels of protection (short-term / long-term)."
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/plugins/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (current stable docs)
- confidence: high

### [2] Freqtrade ships exactly four protection primitives, which cover the four limit types a small live system needs: consecutive-loss lockout, drawdown stop, per-pair profitability lockout, and post-exit cooldown.
- quote: "Available Protections ... `StoplossGuard` Stop trading if a certain amount of stoploss occurred within a certain time window. `MaxDrawdown` Stop trading if max-drawdown is reached. `LowProfitPairs` Lock pairs with low profits `CooldownPeriod` Don't enter a trade right after selling a trade."
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/plugins/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [3] A drawdown limit must be computed on the account equity curve, not on the sum of trade profit ratios — Freqtrade keeps both modes and documents that the ratio-based mode diverges from real account drawdown once position sizing changes over time.
- quote: "With `calculation_mode: \"ratios\"`, drawdown is derived from cumulative trade profit ratios, not from the account equity curve. This is kept for backward compatibility and can differ from account-level drawdown when position sizing changes over time. For new setups, `calculation_mode: \"equity\"` is recommended."
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/plugins/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [4] The daily-loss-limit pattern used in practice is a sliding-window counter of losing stop-outs that triggers a timed halt, and it can be scoped to one side only so that a string of stopped-out longs blocks longs while shorts keep trading.
- quote: "If `trade_limit` or more trades resulted in stoploss, trading will stop for `stop_duration` in minutes (or in candles when using `stop_duration_candles`...)" ... "setting `only_per_side` will make the bot only consider one side, and will then only lock this one side, allowing for example shorts to continue after a series of long stoplosses."
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/plugins/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [5] Per-trade risk budgeting in code is a small set of independent caps whose product bounds gross exposure: max concurrent positions × per-trade stake, an untouchable balance fraction (1% by default, reserved for fees), and a per-bot capital allotment when several bots share one exchange account.
- quote: "`tradable_balance_ratio` Ratio of the total account balance the bot is allowed to trade ... *Defaults to `0.99` 99%).*" ... "`available_capital` Available starting capital for the bot. Useful when running multiple bots on the same exchange account." ... "The maximum capital engaged in trades is `stake_amount * max_open_trades`."
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/configuration/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [6] Sizing must reserve room for the stop itself: the framework inflates the minimum stake by a configurable reserve percentage on top of the stoploss distance, and when it shrinks the final (partial) position it will not go below a configured fraction of the normal stake, to avoid exchange-level order rejection.
- quote: "The bot will reserve `amount_reserve_percent` + stoploss value when calculating min pair stake amount in order to avoid possible trade refusals. *Defaults to `0.05` (5%).*" ... "`last_stake_amount_min_ratio` Defines minimum stake amount that has to be left and executed ... *Defaults to `0.5`.*"
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/configuration/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [7] Perp-specific guards that are cheap to port: keep a configurable buffer between stop and liquidation price, warn on proximity to liquidation as a fraction of the margin-eating move, cancel resting orders when the process dies, and force an emergency market exit after N consecutive exit-order timeouts.
- quote: "`liquidation_buffer` A ratio specifying how large of a safety net to place between the liquidation price and the stoploss to prevent a position from reaching the liquidation price ... *Defaults to `0.05`.*" ... "`liquidation_warn_ratio` Notify when the distance to a position's liquidation stop falls below this fraction of the price move that would use up its margin - with the default of `0.2`, once the stop is less than 2% away at 10x leverage." ... "`unfilledtimeout.exit_timeout_count` How many times can exit orders time out. Once this number of timeouts is reached, an emergency exit is triggered."
- url: https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/configuration/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [8] The canonical regulatory specification of pre-trade risk controls (SEC Rule 15c3-5, adopted 3 Nov 2010, effective 60 days after Federal Register publication with six months to comply) requires four properties that transfer directly to a bot's order gate: automatic pre-trade application, both financial and regulatory checks, sole control by the party placing the order, and periodic review of control effectiveness.
- quote: "Have certain financial and regulatory risk management controls applied automatically on a pre-trade basis before orders route to an exchange or ATS ... Maintain risk management controls and supervisory procedures under the direct and exclusive control of the broker-dealer with market access. ... Establish, document and maintain a system for regularly reviewing the effectiveness of its risk management controls"
- url: https://www.sec.gov/news/press/2010/2010-210.htm
- source_type: primary
- published: 2010-11-03
- confidence: high

### [9] The reference failure case for absent risk limits: on 1 Aug 2012 Knight Capital's router sent more than 4 million orders in 45 minutes while trying to fill 212 customer orders, traded 397 million shares, accumulated several billion dollars of unwanted positions and lost more than $460 million — with the SEC's first-ever 15c3-5 enforcement action following a year later.
- quote: "During the first 45 minutes after the market opened on August 1, Knight Capital's router rapidly sent more than 4 million orders into the market when attempting to fill just 212 customer orders. Knight Capital traded more than 397 million shares, acquired several billion dollars in unwanted positions, and eventually suffered a loss of more than $460 million."
- url: https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222
- source_type: primary
- published: 2013-10-16
- confidence: high

### [10] The specific control defects the SEC found are the ones a small system is most likely to reproduce: aggregate (firm-wide) capital thresholds that cannot actually block orders, and an execution account not wired to the aggregate-exposure control.
- quote: "Relied on financial risk controls that were not capable of preventing the entry of orders that exceeded pre-set capital thresholds for the firm in the aggregate. Did not link the account that received the executions on August 1 to automated controls concerning the firm's overall financial exposure."
- url: https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222
- source_type: primary
- published: 2013-10-16
- confidence: high

### [11] Alerts that are not wired to an automatic action are not controls: Knight's own system emitted 97 error emails identifying the router fault before the open, and nobody acted on them.
- quote: "an internal Knight Capital system generated 97 automated emails that went to a group of personnel. The emails referenced the router and identified an error before the markets opened on August 1. These messages were caused by the code deployment failure, but Knight Capital did not act upon them on August 1."
- url: https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222
- source_type: primary
- published: 2013-10-16
- confidence: high

### [12] Exchange-side leverage caps are not a single number but a tiered function of position notional, and they are retrievable programmatically: Bybit's `GET /v5/market/risk-limit` returns per-tier `riskLimitValue` (position limit), `maintenanceMargin`, `initialMargin`, `maxLeverage`, `isLowestRisk` and `mmDeduction` (the maintenance-margin deduction applied when the tier changes).
- quote: "Query for the risk limit margin parameters." ... "`riskLimitValue` string Position limit ... `maintenanceMargin` number Maintain margin rate ... `initialMargin` number Initial margin rate ... `maxLeverage` string Allowed max leverage ... `mmDeduction` string The maintenance margin deduction value when risk limit tier changed"
- url: https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/risk-limit
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (V5 API docs)
- confidence: high

### [13] Venues make maintenance margin a continuous piecewise-linear function of notional so crossing a tier creates no cliff: maintenance_margin = notional × maintenance_margin_rate − maintenance_deduction, with maintenance_margin_rate(tier n) = (initial margin rate at max leverage at tier n) / 2, i.e. 2.5% at 20x max leverage; concrete mainnet tiers are BTC 40x up to 150M USDC notional then 20x, ETH 25x up to 100M then 15x, and majors (DOGE, LINK, LTC, SUI, UNI, AAVE, ADA, AVAX, BCH, CRV…) 10x below 20M then 5x.
- quote: "`maintenance_margin = notional_position_value * maintenance_margin_rate - maintenance_deduction`" ... "`maintenance_margin_rate(tier = n) = (Initial Margin Rate at Maximum leverage at tier n) / 2` . For example, at 20x max leverage, `maintenance_margin_rate = 2.5%`." ... "BTC ... 0-150M ... 40 ... >150M ... 20"
- url: https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/margin-tiers.md
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [14] Venue leverage is validated only at position open and is not an ongoing enforced invariant, so the bot — not the exchange — must monitor margin usage of a live position; margin may only be removed while remaining margin stays above max(initial margin required, 10% of total position value).
- quote: "Leverage can be set by a user to any integer between 1 and the max leverage. ... Leverage is only checked upon opening a position. Afterwards, the user is responsible for monitoring the leverage usage to avoid liquidation." ... "`transfer_margin_required = max(initial_margin_required, 0.1 * total_position_value)`"
- url: https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/margining.md
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [15] Volatility targeting (scaling exposure down when volatility is high) is not folklore but has documented large alphas and Sharpe-ratio improvements across equity factors and FX carry, which is the empirical justification for inverse-volatility sizing rather than fixed stake.
- quote: "Managed portfolios that take less risk when volatility is high produce large alphas, substantially increase factor Sharpe ratios, and produce large utility gains for mean-variance investors. We document this for the market, value, momentum, profitability, return on equity, and investment factors in equities, as well as the currency carry trade."
- url: https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208
- source_type: primary
- published: 2016-04 (NBER WP 22208; revised 2016-06; published Journal of Finance 72(4):1611–1644, 2017)
- confidence: high
